Conversation cards: printable game and host guide

3–8 people per table · 15–30 minutes

A conversation starter for guests who do not all know each other. There are no points, correct answers or forced personal disclosures.

Make this game

How to play

  1. Cut out the cards and place them face down in the middle of the table. Tell everyone that passing is always allowed.
  2. One person draws a card, reads it aloud and chooses whether to answer first or pass it around.
  3. Let each person speak briefly without interruption. Follow-up questions are welcome if the speaker wants them.
  4. Move to the next card when the conversation naturally finishes. Stop while people are still enjoying it rather than forcing every card.

Ready-to-use pack: Good stories, no pressure

These prompts are included in the generator. Edit any item to suit your guests before printing.

  • What small skill would you like to learn?
  • What meal reminds you of home?
  • What is a place you would revisit?
  • What made you laugh recently?
  • Which everyday invention do you appreciate?
  • What hobby would you try for one afternoon?
  • What is your ideal rainy day?
  • Which song puts you in a good mood?
  • What is something you made yourself?
  • What advice helped you learn something?
  • What would you put in a time capsule?
  • What small thing are you looking forward to?

Set up the printable

One question per line. Six cards fit on each sheet; the text remains exactly as you enter it.

Choose “Make my printables”, inspect every preview sheet, then use “Print / Save PDF”. Player pages appear first and any answer keys follow. In your print dialog, choose only the player page range for guests.

Make it work for your group

For larger events, place a separate pack at each table. For mixed-language groups, invite answers in any shared language and give people time to interpret for one another.

Host notes

Avoid prompts about salary, health, religion or relationship status unless everyone explicitly wants that discussion. A good question gives people control over how much to share.
